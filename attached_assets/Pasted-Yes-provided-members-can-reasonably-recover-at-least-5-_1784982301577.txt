Feature adoption and retention

Do not expose identifiable health behavior or create small audience segments that effectively reveal it.

Features most likely to feel gimmicky
Wellness Score

This can work, but patients must understand exactly what it represents. It should measure consistency, not health:

“Your Wellness Score reflects how consistently you use the habits you selected. It is not a medical assessment.”

Do not penalize someone for failing to enter weight, food, mood, skincare, sleep, and activity every day.

Skin Scan

This is probably the highest-risk feature for disappointment. Lighting, makeup, camera quality, and image processing can produce inconsistent observations.

I would:

Call it an educational “skin check,” not an analysis
Provide photo-taking guidance
Show confidence limitations
Avoid severity ratings, disease-like labels, and before/after promises
Maintain your one-soft-suggestion limit
AI food-photo nutrition estimates

Present calories and macros as approximate ranges, not precise numbers. Otherwise users will interpret confident-looking estimates as factual.

Anonymous community feed

Potentially valuable, but it creates moderation work and can become repetitive. Start with structured posts such as:

“A win I’m proud of…”
“A habit that helped me…”
“Cheer someone on”

Avoid open-ended medical questions, treatment complaints, dosage discussions, and unrestricted photo posting.

Missions, tiers, streaks, challenges, points, Passport, Journey

Individually good; collectively they may feel like six overlapping reward systems. Give each a distinct role:

System	Purpose
Points	Spendable value
Streak	Daily consistency
Missions	Short-term direction
Tiers	Long-term recognition
Beauty Passport	Service discovery
My Journey	Personal memories and progress

If users cannot explain that distinction, simplify the presentation even if the underlying systems remain.

Important privacy red flag

The privacy wall is an excellent design decision, but it should not be described internally as automatically keeping the app “out of HIPAA.” HIPAA applicability depends on the entities, relationships, information flow, and electronic transactions involved—not merely whether staff have a dashboard. HHS states that HIPAA applies to covered providers and individually identifiable health information they maintain or transmit under covered circumstances. HHS HIPAA Privacy Rule

Even if the app falls outside HIPAA, the FTC’s updated Health Breach Notification Rule expressly covers many health apps that maintain identifiable health information from multiple sources. Your combination of self-entered tracking, Oura/phone data, photos, and AI processing makes a formal privacy and security review worthwhile. FTC rule guidance

That does not undermine the product. It means you should have:

A documented data map
Encryption and strict access controls
Vendor and AI-provider review
Clear deletion/export controls
A written breach-response plan
Analytics and advertising SDK review
Health-app-specific legal review before scaling
